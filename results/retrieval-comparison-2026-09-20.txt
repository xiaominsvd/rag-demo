METHODS (same chunks, different scoring)
- Vector: embedding distance, smaller is better (BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5)
- BM25: keyword score, higher is better (jieba tokenization)
- Hybrid: RRF fusion of both top-k lists (rrf_k=60), higher is better

================================================================

  vector: chunk-4, chunk-2, chunk-6

Q3 "What's the difference between RAG and fine-tuning?"
  => All three surface chunk-12 ("not mutually exclusive..."); hybrid

================================================================
CONCLUSIONS
================================================================
1. No method wins everywhere. BM25 owns exact terms/abbreviations
   ("ANN"); vectors handle paraphrase ("prototype" -> "small-scale");
   hybrid usually gets the best of both -- but not always.
2. Hybrid/RRF is not magic. A chunk lukewarm in both lists (2/63) can
   beat a chunk one method is certain about (1/61). Seen at chunk
   sizes 400, 800 and 200 -- a fusion property, not a chunking artifact.
3. Chunking is first-class. The header/body split hid the best answer
   at 400; 800 healed it; 200 fragmented meaning into rankable noise;
   1600 made retrieval pointless. Fix chunking before tuning rankers.
4. Match the embedding model to the corpus language (bge-small-zh on
   English "ANN" ranked essentially at random).
5. On a tiny corpus, skip RAG -- just stuff all docs into the prompt.
   RAG earns its keep only when the corpus doesn't fit the context
   window, which is exactly when chunking granularity starts to matter.

Repo default chunk_size=400 unchanged; sweep was experiment-only
(rebuilt back to 13 chunks afterwards).
